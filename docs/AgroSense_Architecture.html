3. Physical Connection Map & Wiring Instructions

  NodeMCU Pin D1  ──►  Relay Module IN1  (Pump control signal, Active-LOW)
  NodeMCU Pin D2  ──►  Relay Module IN2  (Valve control signal, Active-LOW)
  NodeMCU Pin A0  ──►  Soil Sensor AO    (Analog soil moisture voltage)
  NodeMCU Pin 3V3 ──►  Soil Sensor VCC   (+3.3V power)
  NodeMCU Pin VIN ──►  Relay Module VCC  (+5.0V USB coil power)
  NodeMCU Pin GND ──►  Shared Ground (-) (Common 0V Ground Plane)

4. Power Architecture & Electrical Isolation

To prevent electrical damage, Logic Power (the brain) is separated from Load Power (the motor).

Why Can't the ESP Power a Motor Directly?
  • Current Limitation: The ESP8266 GPIO pin outputs only ~12 mA. A water pump requires 500 to 2000 mA. Connecting a pump directly to a GPIO pin burns out the chip instantly.
  • Inductive Flyback: Motors and solenoids contain wire coils that produce high-voltage reverse electrical spikes (50–200V) when switched off. Optocoupled relays protect the ESP with light-based isolation.

5. Soil Moisture Sensor Physics

The soil sensor measures conductivity between two metallic probes:

  • Dry Soil (0% Moisture): High resistance • Raw ADC reads near 1023 (3.3V).
  • Fully Saturated Soil (100% Moisture): High conductivity • Raw ADC drops to near 350 (1.1V).
  float mapped = (float)map(rawADC, SOIL_DRY_ADC, SOIL_WET_ADC, 0, 100);
  return constrain(mapped, 0.0f, 100.0f);

7. Breadboard Wiring Assembly Guide

Follow these 7 steps with power disconnected:

  1. Ground Bus: Connect NodeMCU GND to the blue (-) ground rail on the breadboard.
  2. +3.3V Bus: Connect NodeMCU 3V3 to the red (+) positive rail on the breadboard.
  3. Soil Sensor Power: Connect Soil Sensor VCC to (+) 3.3V rail, and GND to (-) ground rail.
  4. Soil Sensor Signal: Connect Soil Sensor AO directly to NodeMCU pin A0.
  5. Pump Signal: Connect Relay Module IN1 to NodeMCU pin D1.
  6. Valve Signal: Connect Relay Module IN2 to NodeMCU pin D2.
  7. Relay Power: Connect Relay Module GND to (-) rail, and VCC to NodeMCU VIN (5V).

Instead of hardcoding dynamic DHCP IP addresses, the system resolves agrosense.local automatically:

  💻 Laptop Browser:  "Who is agrosense.local?" (Multicast UDP 5353 query)
  📟 ESP8266 Gateway: "I am agrosense.local, and my IP is 192.168.43.150!"
  💻 Laptop Browser:  "Connecting directly to ws://192.168.43.150:81..."
